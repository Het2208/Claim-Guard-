BEST MODEL SUMMARY — Vehicle Insurance Fraud Detection
=========================================================

FINAL MODEL
------------
HistGradientBoostingClassifier (scikit-learn), inside a single Pipeline:
  FraudCleaner -> ColumnTransformer(winsorize+impute+Yeo-Johnson/scale for
  numerics, impute+one-hot for categoricals) -> HistGradientBoostingClassifier

Hyperparameters (defaults performed as well as or better than any tuned
configuration tested — see model_comparison.csv):
  max_iter=200, learning_rate=0.05, max_leaf_nodes=15,
  l2_regularization=1.0, random_state=42

PREPROCESSING APPROACH
------------------------
See results/preprocessing_summary.txt for full detail. In short: '*'
placeholders and physically-invalid values converted to NaN and imputed
in-pipeline (median / most-frequent, fit on train only); skewed numeric
columns Yeo-Johnson transformed; all numerics winsorized at the 1st/99th
percentile (train-fitted, no rows deleted); categoricals one-hot encoded;
five compact ratio/date features engineered; claim_number and
claim_day_of_week dropped.

IMBALANCE STRATEGY
--------------------
None applied in the final model. class_weight='balanced',
RandomUnderSampler, RandomOverSampler, SMOTENC, and SMOTENC + undersampling
were all tested (results/model_comparison.csv, "3-imbalance/PCA" stage).
None improved fraud PR-AUC or fraud F1 over the plain class distribution;
several increased precision at a real cost to recall. Class imbalance is
instead handled purely via decision-threshold selection (below).

SELECTED THRESHOLD
--------------------
0.18 (probability of fraud), selected by maximizing fraud-class F1 on
5-fold cross-validated out-of-fold predictions on the TRAINING split only
(results/threshold_search.csv). The held-out test set was not used for
threshold selection.

FINAL TEST-SET METRICS (held-out 20%, n=2,399, evaluated once)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  Fraud Precision : 0.282
  Fraud Recall    : 0.946
  Fraud F1        : 0.435
  ROC-AUC         : 0.645
  PR-AUC (Average Precision) : 0.412
  Overall accuracy : 0.395 (low, because the threshold is deliberately
                       tuned toward high recall on the minority class —
                       see caveats below)

  Confusion matrix (rows=actual, cols=predicted; 0=Not Fraud, 1=Fraud):
      [[ 389  1420]
       [  32   558]]

WHY THIS MODEL WAS SELECTED
------------------------------
Across all 38 model/sampling/PCA combinations tested (model_comparison.csv),
gradient-boosted tree models (HistGradientBoosting, LightGBM,
GradientBoosting) consistently topped the ranking on cross-validated
fraud PR-AUC (~0.425-0.435), with the plain, untuned HistGradientBoosting
configuration performing at least as well as every tuned variant, every
resampling variant, and the voting/stacking ensembles built on top of it.
Given that hyperparameter tuning, imbalance handling, and ensembling all
failed to produce a measurable improvement, the simplest, most robust
configuration was selected rather than a more complex one that only adds
variance.

IMPORTANT PREPROCESSING DECISIONS
------------------------------------
- No rows were deleted as outliers; unusual claim amounts are plausible
  fraud signal, so numeric values were capped (winsorized), not removed.
- PCA was evaluated and rejected: it reduced PR-AUC on both a linear and a
  tree-based model and offered no benefit worth the loss of interpretability.
- All preprocessing (imputation, transformation, encoding) is fit only on
  the training data and is bundled inside the saved pipeline, so it is
  never re-fit on the test set or on future data.

HONEST LIMITATIONS (no unsupported claims)
---------------------------------------------
- The dataset's signal is weak and concentrated almost entirely in
  annual_income; nearly every other column is close to uninformative on
  its own (univariate ROC-AUC ~0.50).
- ROC-AUC of 0.645 and PR-AUC of 0.412 (vs. a 0.246 base rate) indicate
  modest discriminative power, not a strong fraud detector.
- The chosen threshold (0.18) trades a large amount of precision for
  recall: roughly 7 out of 10 claims flagged as fraud are not fraud. This
  may or may not be an acceptable trade-off depending on the downstream
  cost of investigating a false positive versus missing real fraud — that
  business decision was not made here, only the threshold curve was
  provided (results/threshold_search.csv) so a different threshold can be
  chosen if the cost trade-off differs.
- This model should NOT be treated as production-ready. It has not been
  validated against a real investigative workflow, has not been checked
  for demographic bias (e.g. its reliance on annual_income), and was
  built and evaluated on a single static CSV extract.
